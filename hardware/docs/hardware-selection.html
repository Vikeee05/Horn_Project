Horn Billing Device

Hardware selection and conceptual schematic

Revision 1.0 | 15 September 2026 | Target form factor: approximately 42 mm watch-dial PCB

Recommended baseline: STM32U575CGU6 + SIMCom A7672G cellular modem + 1-cell Li-ion battery + protected isolated horn-input detector. Use FreeRTOS on the STM32. The cellular modem is independently power-switched because its transmit burst current is much larger than the MCU current.

1. Design decisions

  • Controller: STM32U575CGU6, Cortex-M33, ultra-low-power STM32U5, UFQFPN-48, 3.3 V operation, ample Flash/RAM for FreeRTOS, TLS orchestration and persistent credit logic.
  • Cellular: SIMCom A7672G, LTE Cat 1 bis, UART AT-command interface, TCP/IP and TLS support. This is the preferred modern cellular choice for cloud communication. It is not a 2G-only GSM modem.
  • Horn detection: a dedicated protected digital input from the vehicle horn relay/BCM output, with an optocoupler and timer capture. Measure the active interval in hardware; do not poll the signal in a task.
  • Power: the vehicle input charges a 1-cell Li-ion battery. The battery directly supplies the modem rail through a load switch; an LDO supplies the STM32 and sensors at 3.3 V.
  • Software protocol: horn events are timestamped locally, converted to credits in 250 ms units, queued in nonvolatile storage, and uploaded over MQTT over TLS or HTTPS using the modem AT stack.
Important cellular decision: if the requirement literally means 2G GSM/GPRS, substitute SIM800C. That option is smaller and simpler but depends on legacy 2G networks that are being retired in many countries. Confirm operator coverage before freezing the PCB. A7672G is recommended for a new product where LTE Cat 1 bis is available.

4. Horn detection recommendation

Use a direct discrete horn-line input when the horn relay or BCM output is accessible. The detection path is: vehicle horn signal -> fuse/resistor network -> TVS/RC filter -> optocoupler LED -> optocoupler transistor -> STM32 EXTI/timer pin. This is a signal acquisition circuit, so there is no external communication protocol required.

Configure the timer in input-capture mode. On the first valid rising edge, record a monotonic timestamp; on the falling edge, calculate the duration. Consume credits as floor(duration_ms / 250) while carrying a remainder, or use a product decision to round up. Store the event before attempting cellular transmission.

If the vehicle exposes no usable horn wire, use a qualified automotive CAN transceiver and decode the vehicle's body-control message only after obtaining the vehicle-specific CAN definition. Do not connect an unknown CAN bus directly to the MCU, and do not assume a universal horn CAN identifier.

5. Power and physical design

  • Use a 4-layer rigid PCB, approximately 42 mm round or 40 x 40 mm maximum, with RF and modem areas at the perimeter. A 24 mm modem plus antenna keepout, battery, charger and connectors make a true 35 mm watch dial very difficult.
  • Use a rigid-flex tail for the FPC antenna and vehicle harness if the enclosure requires a circular dial. Do not place copper, battery foil or ground stitching under the antenna keepout specified by the antenna vendor.
  • Route modem VBAT as a short, wide pair. Place at least 470 uF low-ESR bulk capacitance plus 10 uF and 100 nF ceramics close to the modem, subject to the A7672G hardware design guide.
  • Separate the high-current modem return from the MCU/horn-input return until the power-entry star point. Add a modem enable state so the MCU can shut it down between queued sync windows.
  • Use the vehicle supply only through fuse, reverse-polarity protection, TVS and the buck converter. Validate cold crank, load dump, ESD and conducted transients on the target vehicle.

7. Firmware and RTOS partition

FreeRTOS taskResponsibility
HornCaptureTaskConsumes timer edge events, validates pulse duration and posts immutable horn events to a queue.
CreditTaskApplies 250 ms accounting, maintains remainder, commits balance and event sequence to FRAM or internal flash.
CellularTaskOwns the modem UART, registration state, backoff, TLS session and queued event upload.
PowerTaskReads charger/fuel-gauge state and controls modem enable, sleep policy and low-battery behavior.
HealthTaskWatchdog feeding policy, brownout handling, fault counters and diagnostic records.

8. Items still required before PCB release

  • Measure the actual horn signal: voltage range, polarity, pulse behavior and whether the signal is a relay output, BCM output or switch-to-ground line.
  • Confirm cellular band plan, SIM/eSIM choice, operator availability and antenna certification for the deployment country.
  • Choose the exact battery cell and enclosure after modem RF keepout and peak-current testing. Include a certified battery protection and thermal plan.
  • Complete automotive transient, ESD, EMC, vibration, temperature and ingress testing. A watch-sized board mounted in a vehicle still needs automotive-grade protection.
  • Convert this conceptual drawing into a KiCad schematic with exact symbols, net classes, component ratings and vendor-recommended modem reference circuits.
